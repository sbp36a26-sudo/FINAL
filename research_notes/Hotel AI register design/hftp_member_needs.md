## 1. Who HFTP members are, how many there are, where they are, and what they use HFTP for

### Takeaway
HFTP is a nonprofit founded in 1952 and based in Austin, Texas. It serves the finance and technology side of hospitality: controllers, CFOs, IT directors and technology vendors, in hotels and especially in private clubs. Its membership is small, roughly 4,600 to 5,100 depending on the source, but its media reach is much larger: about 100,000 opt-in subscribers across 83 chapters in 46 countries. In Europe it is now building out: it appointed an EU Operations Advisor in 2026 and launched HITEC Paris on 5 Nov 2026.

### Inferences
- The core members are hands-on finance and IT people: controllers, CFOs and IT directors. They are not lawyers. A register tool has to fit how they already work, in spreadsheets and with USALI-style classification, and should speak in cost, control and audit terms rather than legal terms.
- The membership leans towards clubs and the US. European hotel members are probably a small subset, so for European reach the tool will depend on HFTP's media audience (about 100,000 subscribers) and on HITEC Paris rather than on the member base alone.
- Owners, asset managers and GMs are reached through HFTP's media and HITEC more than through membership. No source shows them as a large member group.

## 2. HFTP's offer: tools, certifications, research, courses, publications, events, digital platforms, and how they are delivered

### Takeaway
HFTP delivers through four routes:
- its own websites and member area (USALI, whitepapers, Community@HFTP);
- the HFTP Academy learning platform (academy.hftp.org: CHTP and other certifications, micro-certificates, courses at member prices);
- owned trade media (Hospitality Upgrade, Hotel Online, Clubs Online, HFTP News);
- events (HITEC North America, HITEC Paris, the Annual Convention, and from Feb 2027 the Hospitality AI Exchange).

Since late 2026, AI agents from its partner Inntelo AI answer questions across these properties.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The routes most likely to reach European finance and IT readers are:
  1. a free, downloadable tool (spreadsheet or web page) linked from Hotel Online and Hospitality Upgrade articles;
  2. a session or workshop at HITEC Paris or the Feb 2027 AI Exchange;
  3. an HFTP Academy micro-module built around the register (Academy credit is a reason for members to use it).
- Any tool sitting behind a member login would reach only a few thousand people. An open landing page with optional member extras would reach the roughly 100,000 subscribers.
- Because HFTP already runs AI agents on its sites, the register's content could be written so those agents can answer questions about it, for example short definitions and a FAQ. Whether Inntelo can take in third-party content is unknown.

## 3. What HFTP has published or launched on AI (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
In 2026 HFTP moved quickly to become an AI standard-setter:
- **Feb 2026**: launched an AI Council, focused on governance, ethics, data privacy and education.
- **June 2026 (HITEC)**: renamed it the AI Collective and published a roadmap with five subcommittees, one of them AI Ethics and Governance, plus an AI certificate, data standards and an industry AI portal.
- **Nov 2026**: co-founded the Global Hospitality AI Coordinating Council with HSMAI and AHLA/HTNG.

No published HFTP AI governance guide or template was found. That is the gap a register tool could fill.

### Cited Findings
- **AI Council (Feb 2026)**: formed to set industry standards and expertise. Main goals: ethical AI standards that put data privacy and guest trust first, high-level governance, educational webinars, and acting as an unbiased resource. One headline says participation is limited to HFTP members. — [Hospitality Upgrade](https://www.hospitalityupgrade.com/news/hftp-launches-ai-council-to-establish-industry-standards-and-expertise); [HospitalityNet](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4130808.html); [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/tech/hftp-launches-ai-council) (snippets)

  - Data standards for how hospitality businesses structure and present data, building on existing corporate data-responsibility guidelines for lodging, starting with a gap analysis.

### Inferences
- A Hotel AI Register fits naturally under the AI Collective's **AI Ethics and Governance** subcommittee and could be listed in the planned **industry AI portal**. Those two are the institutional "home" to pitch to the client.
- The new Coordinating Council's stated aim of less duplication and more neutral guidance means the tool should look vendor-neutral and complement the Council, not compete with it. It should also cite the EU AI Act text, not vendor guides.
- The HFTP material found is US-led. None of it covers the EU AI Act specifically. A Europe-specific register covering the EU AI Act, Swiss and UK rules fills a gap HFTP has not filled.

- Status of the AI portal (live or not): unknown.

## 4. Hotel leaders' AI governance pain points in Europe (each figure with its base)

### Takeaway
In Europe the pain is mainly not knowing which AI solutions exist and how to judge them, plus cost, complexity, skills and time. Formal governance is almost absent: about 9% of small, mostly Swiss hotels have a formal strategy that includes AI, and among chains a company-wide strategy is the exception. Privacy and data governance worry hotels, but in European surveys they rank below knowledge and skills. In owner surveys (US-based) privacy and security rank first. No survey found measures hoteliers' awareness of the EU AI Act.

### Cited Findings
**HES-SO Valais-Wallis, Schegg (2025), "AI in European Hotels"**
- Base: survey of hotels in AT, FR, DE, GR, IT and CH, Jan–Apr 2025, done with EHL under the Resilient Tourism project. Coverage gives "over 1,500" hotels; the project's own fact base records about 1,485 respondents, 82% independent, median 31 rooms. It follows a 2023 baseline survey (5 countries, without Italy). — [Schegg 2025 PDF](https://hotelinside.ch/wp-content/uploads/PDF-AI-Adoption-in-European-Hotels-2025.pdf); [HEVS PDF](https://www.hevs.ch/media/document/22/schegg-2025-ai-adoption-in-european-hotels-03072025.pdf) (snippets); base details from the project file `tools/readiness/index.html`, source S1.
- 41% of surveyed hotels use AI. Of AI users, 74% use content-generation tools such as ChatGPT or Gemini, and 44% use online review analysis. The report is inconsistent on whether the 74% is a share of adopters or of all respondents. — [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/european-hotels-show-ai-interest-gaps-remain); [Schegg PDF](https://hotelinside.ch/wp-content/uploads/PDF-AI-Adoption-in-European-Hotels-2025.pdf) (snippets)
- Barriers, % of respondents:
  - poor knowledge of available AI solutions: 39%
  - high setup cost: 35% (61% in the 2023 survey)
  - technical complexity: 34%
  - lack of technical skills: 32%

  Data privacy, integration and ROI concerns were also named (percentages not seen). The number answering the barrier question is not stated. — [Schegg PDF](https://hotelinside.ch/wp-content/uploads/PDF-AI-Adoption-in-European-Hotels-2025.pdf); [Statista summary, Apr 2025](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1626223/hotels-barriers-artificial-intelligence-adoption-europe/) (snippets)
- The report concludes that small hotels need plug-and-play tools and training support, while large ones need data governance frameworks and change management. — same source (snippet)

**HES-SO, Britz & Schegg (Oct 2025), "From Awareness to Action: AI Readiness in Hotels"**
- Base: the survey was sent July–Sep 2025 to 408 hoteliers who had taken part in the earlier survey. A HEVS news post says it is based on 80 hotels, so 408 is probably the number invited and about 80 the number who responded. The sample is 71% Swiss, followed by DE, FR, AT and IT. — [Britz & Schegg PDF](https://www.hevs.ch/media/document/22/britz-schegg-2025-ai-readiness-in-hotels.pdf); [HEVS news, "Beyond the Pilot"](https://www.hevs.ch/en/news/beyond-the-pilot-from-ai-experiments-to-hotel-transformation-213102) (snippets)
- Findings:
  - 35% test AI tools case by case;
  - main barriers are lack of time and budget, limited technical expertise, and difficulty evaluating AI providers;
  - data protection concerns and staff resistance "play a smaller role";
  - the authors call for internal capacity-building, data governance and clear, evidence-based ROI tools.

  The project's fact base also records that 9% have a formal strategy that includes AI (base about 79 hotels). — same sources (snippets); 9% figure from project file `tools/readiness/index.html` (ASN-002-01), not re-checked here.

**h2c, hotel chains, global (vendor-sponsored, unweighted)**

  - 7% have a comprehensive, company-wide AI strategy. Secondary coverage also reports 6% and 8% for this item, so the wording differs between articles;

**Discarded figure**

### Inferences
- The register's first job is not compliance as such but **making things visible**. Most European hotels do not know what AI they already run, often tools bundled inside their PMS, RMS, CRM or chatbot. An inventory that shows "you already use AI here" addresses the top barrier, poor knowledge of solutions (39%).
- Skills (32% in HES-SO; 56% among h2c chains) and time are the binding limits. The tool needs to be fillable in under about an hour by a controller or GM, without AI expertise.
- Owners and asset managers (Wyndham: privacy and security 46%, cost 42%) want a one-page view of risk and cost per property, not a legal analysis.
- With only 11% of chains holding a dedicated AI budget and 13% reporting measurable returns, a register that records cost, owner and expected benefit per AI system would give finance members the ROI tracking they currently lack.

### Gaps
- No survey found that measures European hoteliers' awareness of, or readiness for, the EU AI Act (percent aware, percent with an AI inventory, percent with an AI policy).
- No 2025–2026 hospitality-specific AI governance survey found from Skift Research, Oracle, Amadeus, Deloitte or McKinsey. These may exist but did not appear in searches.
- Accor, IHG, Marriott, Hilton, Meliá, NH/Minor and Scandic statements on AI governance: not searched within this tool-call budget.
- The full PDFs of Schegg (2025) and Britz & Schegg (2025) could not be opened from this session (host blocked). The n per question needs checking against the originals.

## 5. What would make the tool valuable and adopted, and what makes members ignore tools

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on what makes members adopt or ignore tools is thin. The survey findings above point to a short, plug-and-play, vendor-neutral register that helps with budgets and audits and needs no AI knowledge. The likely reasons members would ignore it are complexity, needing a login, looking like legal advice, and looking vendor-driven.

### Cited Findings
- Small hotels need "plug-and-play tools and training support"; large ones need "data governance frameworks and change management". — [Schegg 2025](https://hotelinside.ch/wp-content/uploads/PDF-AI-Adoption-in-European-Hotels-2025.pdf) (snippet)
- Hotels need "clear, evidence-based ROI tools", and difficulty evaluating AI providers is a main barrier. — [Britz & Schegg 2025](https://www.hevs.ch/media/document/22/britz-schegg-2025-ai-readiness-in-hotels.pdf) (snippet)
- Integration with existing systems is the top AI purchase criterion for 70% of chains (h2c 2025, 171 chains). — project file `tools/readiness/index.html` (NEX-188-15), citing [HospitalityNet h2c release](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4129121/h2c-unveils-landmark-global-study-on-ai-automation-in-hospitality)
- HFTP's partners present their AI work as "no-sales" and as guidance for professionals "not sales targets". ### Inferences
These are design hypotheses to test in the team's interviews, not findings.
- **Value drivers, ranked for HFTP's finance and IT audience:**
  1. a board- and audit-ready one-page export (per hotel and per portfolio);
  2. an AI line in the budget: cost per system, with renewal dates and vendor contract terms;
  3. a vendor due-diligence checklist for contract negotiations: data use, model updates, AI Act role as provider or deployer, liability and exit;
  4. clarity on who owns which AI system and its data under owner/operator or franchise contracts;
  5. a timeline view of AI Act dates, flagged "subject to the Omnibus", rather than a hard-coded 2 Dec 2027;
  6. evidence for insurers, for example cyber or AI cover questionnaires. No source was found that insurers ask hotels for an AI inventory.
- **Reasons members ignore tools:**
  - too many fields;
  - needing a login or account;
  - US-only framing;
  - legal jargon, or fear that filling it in counts as legal advice or creates a written record that could be used against them;
  - a vendor logo on the tool;
  - no clear owner or update date.
- **Adoption levers:**
  - works offline as a file (Excel or a single HTML page) with nothing to install;
  - pre-filled with common hotel AI systems by department (the team's D4 catalogue can feed this);
  - USALI-style department names, familiar to controllers;
  - an Academy micro-credit or a HITEC Paris workshop as the reason to start.

## 6. Branding, IP and liability when a student project delivers a tool to an industry association

### Takeaway
No source specific to student projects was found. The points below are practical considerations drawn from general practice and HFTP's own positioning. They are not legal advice and should be confirmed with EHL (the project agreement) and HFTP.

### Cited Findings
- HFTP and its new Council present their guidance as neutral and non-commercial (see section 3). That raises the bar for anything carrying HFTP branding. — [Asian Hospitality](https://www.asianhospitality.com/hospitality-groups-unite-on-ai-guidance/) (snippet)
- HFTP already runs an AI Collective with an Ethics and Governance subcommittee and an AI portal in development. A student tool would need to fit into, or be cleared by, that structure. — [Hospitality Upgrade](https://www.hospitalityupgrade.com/news/hftps-ai-collective-solidifies-roadmap-to-provide-industry-wide-resources-2) (snippet)
- AHLA/HTNG state that their work is limited to "technical standards, education, and lawful advocacy". Associations are careful about the scope of what they publish. — [AHLA/HTNG T100](https://www.ahla.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/AHLA-HTNG-T100-Top-Industry-Technology-Challenges.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
These are considerations to raise with the client and coach.
- **IP and ownership:** check the EHL SBP agreement for who owns the deliverables (students, EHL or client). Agree in writing, as a scope item validated by client, coach and team, that HFTP receives the tool with the right to rebrand and change it.
- **Branding:** do not put the HFTP logo on it unless HFTP approves. Ship it neutral, for example "prepared for HFTP by EHL SBP Group 36", and let HFTP rebrand it through the AI Collective.
- **Liability:** add a clear "information tool, not legal advice; check with counsel" notice, an "as at" date for every legal reference, and links to the official AI Act text. Avoid any automated "you are compliant" verdict; use "items to check" instead.
- **Maintenance:** the rules are changing (the Omnibus and the high-risk dates). Name an owner after hand-over, most likely the AI Collective's Ethics and Governance subcommittee. Keep the legal dates in one editable data table and set a review cadence.
- **Data:** if the tool stays a local file, HFTP never holds hotel data, which avoids GDPR controller questions. A hosted version would need a privacy notice and a decision on who the data controller is.

### Gaps
- No published HFTP policy on third-party or student-contributed tools or content was found.
- The EHL SBP contract terms on IP: not accessible from here; ask the coach.
