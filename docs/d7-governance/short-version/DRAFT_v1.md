### Page 2: the AI Act in one minute (design: A1 "The short version", p. 6)
**The AI Act has no special rules for hotels.**¹ A tool counts as high-risk because of what it does, not where it is used.
Most AI in hotels carries light duties.
The heavy duties sit in four places:
1. **The hotel as employer:** tools that hire, schedule, score or watch staff
2. **Biometrics:** recognising guests or staff by face or voice, or reading their emotions
3. **Telling guests:** chatbots, voice agents and realistic AI images
4. **Becoming the maker by accident:** building, rebranding or repurposing a tool
*More: A1, p. 6*

### Page 3: five labels (design: A2 "The five labels", p. 7)
Every AI tool falls under one label, depending on what it is used for.¹
- **Prohibited**, since 2 Feb 2025: banned. *Example:* a tool that reads staff or candidates' emotions from face or voice.³
- **High-risk**, from 2 Dec 2027: on the Act's list (hiring and managing staff, biometrics, credit). *Example:* recruiting AI; scoring reservation agents' calls.
- **High-risk if…**, from 2 Dec 2027: depends on set-up. *Example:* a rota tool that ranks staff on speed or ratings, but not one that allocates by availability `draft guidance`⁸
- **Transparency**, since 2 Aug 2026: people must know it is AI.² *Example:* guest chatbot or phone agent.
- **Minimal**, no date: no duty beyond AI literacy. *Example:* dynamic pricing, forecasting, review analysis.
*More: A2, p. 7 · by department: A6, p. 14–16*

### Page 4: five things to do now (design: "Start here", p. 4, the five cards on one page, titles only)
Five checks cover most of what the AI Act already asks of a hotel, and none needs a lawyer.
1. **List every AI tool in use:** what it does, who supplies it, who is responsible. *GM; head office pools the lists*
2. **Check every guest chatbot and phone agent says it is AI at the start.** *GM, with the vendor* (required since 2 Aug 2026)¹
3. **Stop CVs or staff data being pasted into general chatbots to rank people.** *GM and HR* `our reading` GDPR
4. **Ask rota, housekeeping, call-centre and camera vendors whether the tool ranks staff, reads emotions or recognises faces or voices; switch off what you do not need.** *Department heads, with IT or purchasing*
5. **Brief staff who use AI and keep a note of who was briefed.** *GM and HR* (required since 2 Feb 2025; no certificate needed)¹ ⁴
Labour: before any tool that schedules, scores or watches staff, involve the works council or staff representatives.⁵ ⁶
*More: p. 4–5*

## Draft 2: "Your role on one page"
4 pages after a cover · about 1,000 words in all, about 250 per page · 1 to 2 minutes for your own page · design reused from
B1 (p. 19–20): "What it means for you", "Watch for" (3), "Do" (3), plus a "Read next" line

### Page 1: owners and asset managers (design: B1, p. 19–20)
**What it means for you.** An owner is rarely the AI Act user of a hotel's tools; the operator or the brand usually is (A4). But owners
carry the money risk: they approve the spend, sign the agreements and hold the asset a fine, a stopped rollout or a data breach can hit.
**Watch for**
- **Staff technology is the main high-risk spend.** Recruiting tools, and rota or monitoring tools that rank staff, become high-risk on 2 Dec 2027.¹
- **Who pays is a contract question.** The AI Act says nothing about who pays for compliance or fines between owner, operator and brand.
- **Rollouts can be stopped.** Labour In Germany and France, courts can halt staff tools where staff representatives were skipped.⁵ ⁶
**Do**
1. Ask the operator for the **list of AI tools** in your hotels: what each does, who supplies it, its label.
2. Check **management and franchise agreements:** who is the user of each tool, who is the maker of anything the group builds, who pays for compliance and fines.
3. Approve staff technology with **2 Dec 2027 in the business case**, and ask vendors to confirm CE marking and registration by then.
*Read next: B1, p. 19–20 · who is responsible, A4, p. 11 · fines, A7, p. 17*

### Page 2: brand and head office (CIO) (design: B2, p. 21–22)
**What it means for you.** Head office decides what is bought, built and rolled out, so one decision there sets the exposure of every
property. It is also where a group most easily becomes the maker.
**Watch for**
- **Becoming the maker.** A group chatbot built on a large language model makes the group its maker, so the group owes the "you are talking to AI" notice.¹ ²
- **Brand standards can make the brand a user** where franchise or management standards impose a tool and its settings (A4).
- **Assistants and agents are judged as a whole:** if a chain of AI agents together ranks staff or screens applicants, the whole is high-risk `draft guidance`⁸
**Do**
1. Keep a **group register** of AI tools, fed by each hotel's list.
2. Publish a short list of **approved AI tools with work accounts**, so staff do not turn to personal chatbots.⁴⁰
3. Use the **seven vendor questions** (p. 12) in every purchase.
*Read next: B2, p. 21–22 · maker or user, A3, p. 8–10 · vendor questions, p. 12*

### Page 3: general managers (design: B3, p. 23–24)
**What it means for you.** At property level the hotel is almost always the user of its AI tools. The user's duties are short, but they
are the property's own, and the five checks under "Start here" are yours.
**Watch for**
- **What is already banned:** any tool that reads staff or candidates' emotions from face or voice, including "agent sentiment" scores on calls.¹ ³
- **What guests must be told:** a chatbot or phone agent must say it is AI at the start; a line in the terms is not enough.²
- **Shadow AI:** staff pasting guest or staff data into personal chatbots. GDPR A paste can be a data breach.⁹
**Do**
1. Name **one person responsible for each tool** on the property's AI list.
2. Before switching on any tool that schedules, scores or watches staff, **involve HR, the data protection officer and staff representatives.**
3. Keep **one incident plan** for AI-related data leaks (the EU 72-hour rule; Swiss and UK reporting where relevant).
*Read next: B3, p. 23–24 · five things to do now, p. 4–5*

### Page 4: department heads (design: B-page frame with the C-page labels)
**What it means for you.** Most AI in your department carries light duties. The risk is a feature that judges individual staff, reads
emotions or recognises people.
| Department | What to watch | Read |
|---|---|---|
| HR | Hiring and judging staff is high-risk; reading candidates' emotions is prohibited³ | C1, p. 25 |
| Rooms division | Housekeeping tools that rank or report on individual attendants `draft guidance`⁸ | C2, p. 28 |
| Commercial | Voice agents with emotion or voiceprint features; personalised prices; AI images | C3, p. 30 |
| Food and beverage | Food-waste cameras used to rate individual cooks `draft guidance`⁸ | C4, p. 32 |
| Security and IT | Cameras that match guests' faces (high-risk) or read staff emotions (prohibited)³ | C5, p. 34 |
| Maintenance, procurement, finance, legal | Mostly minimal; safety AI in lifts or gas equipment is the maker's duty from 2 Aug 2028 | C6, p. 36 |
**Do (every department)**
1. Ask each vendor whether the tool **ranks, scores or monitors individual staff, reads emotions or recognises faces or voices**, and switch off what you do not need.
2. Check that every tool that talks to guests **says it is AI** at the start.
3. Labour Before a tool that measures staff work, involve staff representatives.⁵ ⁶
