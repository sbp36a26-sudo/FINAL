Hotel AI use-case catalogue by department

What AI does in each of the 15 hotel departments, grouped into four areas. Pick an area, then a department: how AI works there, which tasks it takes over, where it supports staff, what it adds, what it does not solve yet, and every use case.

How to read this page

Automates

AI does the task from start to finish; staff handle exceptions.

Assists

Staff still decide or deliver; AI drafts, recommends, forecasts or analyses.

Adds new

A task or capability nobody did before, or not at this scale.

Not solved yet

What no credible source shows AI doing, or what is still missing.

The 15 departments at a glance

Click a department to open it.

Hotel groups: AI initiatives announced by the 41 European hotel groups researched (see the Hotel groups tab), split by whether the group built the AI itself or with a partner, or bought it from a vendor; n/s = partner not stated. Company and press claims, not proof of use.

Area level: High when at least half of the area's use cases are at Medium or High, Medium from 30%, Low below that (team rule). Department level: the highest level among its use cases.

Use levels: High = independent data show wide use or it is standard in mainstream products; Medium = on sale with named hotel customers; Low = a few named hotels or pilots; Very low = prototypes or announcements. Hotel adoption overall: 41% of 1,485 hotels in six countries use AI S1; 21.31% of EU accommodation firms with 10+ staff O17; 26% of 2,713 hotels in 28 countries, base not stated A2. Different samples: not a trend.

Companies offering AI for each department

The companies whose own website or press release states the AI function for hotels, checked 10–11 Nov 2026. What each one offers is the company's own description, condensed: a company claim, not evidence that hotels use it. Numbers in black mark the top 10 in the team's evidence-based ranking.

What hotel groups are doing with AI

European hotel groups from the team's list, researched on the public web on 11 Nov 2026. Each entry is a named AI initiative with its source; "Says" shows who makes the claim. Company and vendor announcements are claims, not proof of results. A group with few entries may simply publish little.

At a glance

Own AI: "Builds its own" = an in-house team, assistant or model is documented; "Buys from vendors" = only bought products found; "Both" = both documented; "Not clear" = public sources do not show it.
